District of West Vancouver · Council term 2022–2026

How each Councillor voted

Independent summary, not affiliated with the District of West Vancouver. Every Council vote from November 2022 to August 2026, rebuilt from the District of West Vancouver's official meeting minutes (117 meetings). West Vancouver doesn't publish a voting table, so each vote was read from the minutes, which name anyone who voted against and anyone absent at the vote. Routine motions such as adopting the agenda or adjourning are left out.

Summary

Key findings

  • Most votes are unanimous, but less so than at the City. 85% of votes (973 of 1,145) had no one opposed. That's between the City of North Vancouver (93%) and the District of North Vancouver (78%).
  • Mayor Mark Sager and Scott Snider anchor the majority. They almost never vote no (4 and 16 times across the term), agree on 89% of split votes and are on the winning side more than 90% of the time. Peter Lambur and Sharon Thompson usually join them.
  • Christine Cassidy is Council's most frequent dissenter by far, with 116 “no” votes (10% of her votes) and 26 votes where she was the only one opposed. She is on the losing side of 61% of split votes. Most of her “no” votes are on housing and development: the Cypress Village and Eagleridge development, Ambleside Local Area Plan apartment rezonings, OCP amendments and the provincially required small-scale housing zoning in April 2024.
  • Linda Watt is Cassidy's closest ally, but also votes with the majority. Watt agrees with Cassidy on 58% of split votes and with Lambur on 71%. Cassidy, Lambur and Watt voted against each reading of the Cypress Village and Eagleridge bylaws, which still passed 4–3.
  • Nora Gambioli has an independent streak. She was the only “no” 17 times, on a wide mix: the 2023 and 2024 budgets, drinking in public places, new Council committees and the 29th Street staircase.
  • Pay parking, trees and transit split Council in unusual ways. Snider and Thompson opposed parts of the stronger tree protection bylaw. A proposal to pause pay parking in Ambleside Park was defeated 5–2, with Sager and Snider in favour. TransLink-funded bus speed and reliability projects were defeated 4–2.

How each member votes, by category

Categories are assigned from keywords in each motion's title and text, since West Vancouver's minutes don't tag topics. Each cell shows "no" votes out of votes cast in that category. Darker cells mean a higher share of "no" votes. A "no" is a vote against one specific motion, which is often an amendment, a referral or a motion to reject something, so these counts don't show whether a member supports or opposes the topic itself. Open the votes to see what each motion said. A vote can belong to more than one category: a rezoning for a six-storey rental building counts under both Housing & density and Development. Method

How to read this

Source. The District of West Vancouver's Council meeting minutes: 117 sets of Regular, Special, Inaugural and Committee of the Whole minutes from 7 November 2022 to 24 August 2026, retrieved 28 September 2026. The District's meeting list only goes back to January 2024; earlier minutes were retrieved from the same site by their file names. Public hearings are not included because Council doesn't vote at them. Each vote links to its minutes and, where available, the meeting video.

How votes were counted. West Vancouver records each result as “CARRIED” or “DEFEATED”, followed by the names of anyone who opposed (older minutes say “voted in the negative”) and anyone absent at the vote. Members named as opposed are counted against, members absent from the meeting or at the vote are counted absent, and everyone else is counted for.

“Against” means against that motion. Amendments, deferrals and motions to pause or reject something are separate votes, and a “no” on them can mean support for the underlying item. Expand any row to read the motion.

Consent agendas. Council often approves a bundle of routine items in one vote. When that vote was unanimous, each item is listed separately. When someone opposed the bundle, it appears as a single vote, because the minutes don't say which item they objected to.

Limits. Each reading of a bylaw is its own vote, so a contested bylaw can appear three or four times. Reading minutes by machine can miss unusual wording; the linked minutes are the official record.

Categories. Each vote is sorted by keywords in its title and motion text. A vote can sit in several categories, and some placements are judgment calls.

Privacy. Names of residents who spoke at meetings or made requests are removed from the motion text here.

How this page was made. The votes were read from the official records by software and checked against samples of the originals. The summaries and topic categories were written and assigned with AI assistance, and they can contain mistakes. Where a summary and the vote log differ, the vote log and the linked official records are correct.
