How to read a council vote

A short explanation of the terms used on this site. It describes how B.C. councils generally work. Each council has its own procedure bylaw, and the official minutes are always the authoritative record.

The parts of a vote

Motion
A proposal put to Council for a decision, usually beginning with “THAT”. Council decides things by voting on motions. This site shows the wording of each motion where the source provides it.
Moved by
The member who formally put the motion on the floor. Moving a motion is a step in the meeting. It does not by itself show who wrote the proposal: many motions adopt a staff recommendation, and routine bylaw readings are often moved by whoever is next. Some councils’ minutes do not name the mover at all. This site never guesses the mover.
Seconded by
A second member who agreed the motion should be debated. Seconding does not necessarily mean the member supported the motion in the end.
Carried / Defeated
Carried means the motion passed. Defeated means it did not. A tied vote is defeated. “Carried unanimously” means no member voted against.
In favour, Against, Absent, No recorded vote
“Absent” means the member was not in the room for that vote, which includes a member who stepped out after declaring a conflict of interest. “No recorded vote” means the source does not show a vote for that member, for example because they were not yet on Council. Many councils’ minutes list only the members who voted against; everyone else present is counted as in favour, which is also how B.C. law generally treats a member who is present and does not vote.

Types of motion

Main motion
The proposal itself, for example to approve a permit, adopt a bylaw or direct staff to do something.
Amendment
A motion to change the wording of a motion before Council votes on it. Council votes on the amendment first, then on the main motion (called “as amended” if the amendment carried). A vote against an amendment is not a vote against the main motion, and a member can vote for an amendment and still vote against the final motion.
Referral
A motion to send an item to staff or a committee for more information or work, instead of deciding it now. It is not a decision for or against the item.
Postponement or deferral
A motion to put off the decision to a later meeting.
Reconsideration
A motion to bring back a matter Council has already voted on, so it can be voted on again.
Procedural
Motions about running the meeting or handling paperwork, such as receiving a report for information. On most pages, routine housekeeping votes (adopting the agenda, adjourning) are left out.

Other terms

Consent agenda
A list of routine items approved together in one vote, without debate. A member can usually ask for an item to be taken off the list and voted on separately.
Bylaw readings
A bylaw normally passes through first, second and third reading and then adoption, sometimes over several meetings, and some bylaws go to a public hearing along the way. Each stage can be a separate vote. A vote for an early reading can be a vote to let the bylaw move to the next stage, so read which stage a vote was for.
Several votes on one item
Council often votes more than once on the same agenda item: an amendment, a referral that fails, then the main motion. Where this site can tell, each vote is labelled, for example “Vote 2 of 3”, and a link shows them all in order.
Closed meetings
Councils may meet in private for certain matters such as land, legal and staffing issues. Votes at closed meetings are not public and are not on this site.

About NO votes

A NO vote applies to the specific motion shown. It does not necessarily mean the councillor opposed the underlying project or issue. A member may vote against an amendment because they prefer the original wording, against a referral because they want a decision now, or against a motion because of one clause in it. Read the motion text, and the minutes or video if you need the reasons.

This site shows factual tallies and the text of each motion. It does not rate members or describe them as supporters or opponents of anything.
