City of Vancouver · Council term 2022–2026

How each Councillor voted

Independent summary, not affiliated with the City of Vancouver. Every recorded Council vote from November 2022 to August 2026, from the City of Vancouver's official council voting records on its open data portal, which lists how each member voted. Thirteen people served on Council this term: Christine Boyle and Adriane Carr left, and Lucy Maloney and Sean Orr joined after the April 2025 by-election. In the vote log, a dash means the member wasn't on Council at the time, or abstained or declared a conflict.

Summary

Key findings

  • Vancouver's Council splits more often than most. 79% of votes (1,912 of 2,414) were unanimous, close to the District of North Vancouver and well below the other three councils here. 187 motions failed, many of them amendments from the minority.
  • Mayor Ken Sim's ABC Vancouver majority votes as a bloc. Sim, Lisa Dominato, Sarah Kirby-Yung, Mike Klassen, Peter Meiszner, Brian Montague and Lenny Zhou agree on 87–98% of split votes and are on the winning side 89–97% of the time.
  • The minority also votes together, and usually loses. Pete Fry, Adriane Carr and Christine Boyle agreed on 90–95% of split votes, and after the by-election Fry, Lucy Maloney and Sean Orr agreed on 77–87%. They are on the winning side of only 16–25% of split votes.
  • Rebecca Bligh changed sides partway through the term. Until mid-2024 she voted with Mayor Sim on 97% of split votes. Since then it's about a third, and she now votes mostly with Fry, Maloney and Orr.
  • Brian Montague is the majority's most independent member. He was the only “no” 24 times, more than anyone else, including against parts of Council's strategic priorities and the Broadway Plan review.
  • Budgets and major policies split along the same line. Boyle, Carr and Fry voted against the budgets, and after the by-election the majority and the minority split on the 2026 budget, the DTES directions, the proposed overdose prevention site and a new City-owned business corporation.
  • Attendance. Mayor Sim missed 86 sittings, most of them public hearings. Absent members aren't counted in the “against” rates.

Method

How to read this

Source. The City of Vancouver's Council voting records dataset, retrieved 3 October 2026. It covers Council, standing committee, public hearing, special Council and Auditor General Committee votes from 7 November 2022 to 14 August 2026, with each member's vote recorded by the City. Each vote links to its meeting page, which has the agenda, minutes and video.

How votes are counted. The City records each member as in favour, in opposition, absent, abstaining, declaring a conflict, or not voting. Abstentions and conflicts are shown here as no vote and listed with the item; under BC law an abstention is counted as a vote in favour.

“Against” means against that motion. Amendments, referrals and motions to reject something are separate votes with the same agenda title, and a “no” on them can mean support for the underlying item. Open the meeting page to see the exact wording.

No motion text. The dataset gives the agenda item and outcome but not the wording of each motion, so several votes on one item (amendments, then the main motion) can share a title.

Categories. Each vote is sorted by keywords in its agenda title. A vote can sit in several categories, and some placements are judgment calls.
