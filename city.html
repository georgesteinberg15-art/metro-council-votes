City of North Vancouver · Council term 2022–2026

How each Councillor voted

Independent summary, not affiliated with the City of North Vancouver. Every Council vote from November 2022 to September 2026, rebuilt from the City's official meeting minutes (115 sets). The City doesn't publish a voting table, so each vote was read from the minutes, which record who was present and name anyone who voted against. Routine motions such as adopting the agenda or adjourning are left out.

Summary

Key findings

  • This Council agrees far more often than the District's. 93% of votes (940 of 1,008) were unanimous. Only 68 were split, compared with 22% split votes at the District.
  • A steady majority: Mayor Linda Buchanan, Angela Girard and Jessica McIlroy. On split votes they vote together 92–98% of the time and are on the winning side 83–88% of the time. Tony Valente usually sides with them (65–70% agreement).
  • Don Bell and Shervin Shahriari cast the most “no” votes (about 3% of their votes each) and agree with each other 68% of the time on split votes. They end up on the losing side of about 40% of split votes. Bell was the only “no” on 10 votes, 9 of them rezonings or zoning bylaws, including 120-128 East 14th Street, 2416 Western Avenue and 648 West 14th Street. Shahriari stood alone against the rental projects at 144 West 21st Street and 1540 St. Georges Avenue.
  • Holly Back is the swing vote. She agrees with every other member between 55% and 63% of the time on split votes. She joined Bell and Shahriari against the Curb Access and Parking Plan, the St. Andrews Avenue safety project, the Eastside Connector, the Lonsdale Great Street Plan and the 2025 revised financial plan. Each of those carried 4–3.
  • Tony Valente stood alone against the City's provincially required OCP and zoning changes in November and December 2025 (8 votes across the readings and adoption).
  • Transportation and parking is the sharpest dividing line for its size. Housing and development produce the most split votes overall. Budgets, public services and governance are almost always unanimous.

Method

How to read this

Source. The City of North Vancouver's Council meeting minutes: 115 sets of Regular, Special, Inaugural and Workshop minutes from 7 November 2022 to 21 September 2026, retrieved 28 September 2026. Each vote links to the minutes it came from.

How votes were counted. The City doesn't publish a voting table. Its minutes record each result as “CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY” or as “CARRIED”/“DEFEATED” with the names of those opposed. Members named as opposed are counted against; everyone else present is counted for. Attendance comes from each meeting's present and absent list, adjusted when a member joined late, left early or declared a conflict of interest.

“Against” means against that motion. Amendments, deferrals and motions to reject something are separate votes, and a “no” on them can mean support for the underlying item. Expand any row to read the motion.

Limits. When a member stepped out briefly and returned, the minutes don't say which votes they missed, so they're counted as present. Six votes are recorded only as “CARRIED” with no names; these are counted as having no opposition and marked in the log. Each reading of a bylaw is its own vote, so a contested bylaw can appear three or four times.

Categories. Each vote is sorted by keywords in its title and motion text. A vote can sit in several categories, and some placements are judgment calls.

Privacy. Names of individual applicants are removed from the motion text here. The linked minutes are the full official record.
