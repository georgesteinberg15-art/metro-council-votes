City of New Westminster · Council term 2022–2026

How each Councillor voted

Independent summary, not affiliated with the City of New Westminster. Every Council vote from November 2022 to September 2026, rebuilt from the City of New Westminster's official meeting minutes (112 meetings). New Westminster doesn't publish a voting table, so each vote was read from the minutes, which name anyone who opposed. Routine motions such as adopting the agenda, extending the meeting or approving the consent agenda as a bundle are left out.

Summary

Key findings

  • New Westminster has the most divided council on this site. Only 74% of votes (1,135 of 1,541) were unanimous, and 124 motions failed.
  • A five-member majority: Mayor Patrick Johnstone with Ruby Campbell, Tasha Henderson and Nadine Nakagawa, usually joined by Jaimie McEvoy. Johnstone, Campbell, Henderson and Nakagawa agree on 88–93% of split votes and are on the winning side 91–95% of the time.
  • Daniel Fontaine and Paul Minhas vote together 94% of the time and cast by far the most “no” votes (about 220 each, roughly 15% of their votes). They are on the losing side of 80% of split votes, and many of their own motions were defeated 5–2.
  • Jaimie McEvoy is the majority's most independent member. He agrees with the others on about 79% of split votes and was the only “no” 11 times, including on the regional ride-hailing business licence.
  • Budgets and taxes split the same way every year. Financial plans and tax rate bylaws passed 5–2 with Fontaine and Minhas opposed. Their motions to cut or pause the 3.5% Climate Action Levy on the City's electrical utility were defeated.
  • Other recurring flashpoints include the Good Neighbour Agreement for the Health Contact Centre at 40 Begbie Street, supportive housing, crime and public safety motions, cooling equipment in rental units and the City's rebranding.
  • Attendance. McEvoy missed 20 meetings and Henderson 10. Absent members aren't counted in the “against” rates.

How each member votes, by category

Categories are assigned from keywords in each motion's title and text, since New Westminster's minutes don't tag topics. Each cell shows "no" votes out of votes cast in that category. Darker cells mean a higher share of "no" votes. A "no" is a vote against one specific motion, which is often an amendment, a referral or a motion to reject something, so these counts don't show whether a member supports or opposes the topic itself. Open the votes to see what each motion said. A vote can belong to more than one category: a rezoning for a six-storey rental building counts under both Housing & density and Development. Method

How to read this

Source. The City of New Westminster's Council meeting minutes: 112 sets of Council, Special Council, Council-following-public-hearing and Inaugural minutes from 7 November 2022 to 21 September 2026, retrieved 3 October 2026. Three meetings have no minutes posted. Each vote links to its minutes and, where available, the meeting video.

How votes were counted. New Westminster records results as “CARRIED” or “DEFEATED” with the names of anyone opposed, either on the next line or in a sentence such as “it was CARRIED with Cllrs. Fontaine and Minhas opposed.” Members named as opposed are counted against; everyone else present is counted for. Attendance comes from each meeting's present list, adjusted when a member declared a conflict and left, or returned.

“Against” means against that motion. Amendments, referrals and individual parts of a motion are often voted separately, and a “no” on them can mean support for the underlying item. Expand any row to read the motion.

Limits. When a motion was split into parts, some parts are listed only as “the question on Part 2 of the main motion”; the linked minutes have the full wording. Motions to extend a meeting are left out because they need more than a simple majority. Each reading of a bylaw is its own vote.

Categories. Each vote is sorted by keywords in its title and motion text. A vote can sit in several categories, and some placements are judgment calls.

Privacy. Names of residents who spoke at meetings are removed from the motion text here.

How this page was made. The votes were read from the official records by software and checked against samples of the originals. The summaries and topic categories were written and assigned with AI assistance, and they can contain mistakes. Where a summary and the vote log differ, the vote log and the linked official records are correct.

Corrections. If something here looks wrong, whether a vote, a summary or a category, please email georgesteinberg15@gmail.com with the date and item. Corrections from council members, staff and residents are all welcome and will be checked against the official record.
