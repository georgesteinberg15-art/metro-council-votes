About NO votes

A NO vote applies to the specific motion shown. It does not necessarily mean the councillor opposed the underlying project or issue. Council frequently votes on amendments, referrals, timing changes and procedural motions before voting on the final proposal. Read the motion text before interpreting a NO vote.

About this data

Interpreting voting records

Voting records show how members voted on individual motions, but they do not by themselves show who developed or introduced an initiative. Where the minutes name them, this site therefore also identifies the councillor who moved and seconded each motion.

A vote against an amendment, referral or procedural motion should also not automatically be interpreted as opposition to the underlying issue.

The site presents the motion text and official minutes so users can review the context themselves. It does not rate members or describe them as supporters or opponents of an issue.

Source

The City of Coquitlam's Council meeting archive: 98 sets of Regular, Special and Inaugural Council minutes from 7 November 2022 to 21 September 2026, retrieved 7 October 2026. Each vote links to its minutes and the meeting video.

The official record is authoritative. Where this page and the official record differ, the official record is correct.

How votes were counted

Coquitlam records each result as “CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY”, “CARRIED” or “DEFEATED”, followed by a line naming any member who “registered opposition”. Members named there are counted against; everyone else present is counted in favour. Attendance comes from each meeting's opening paragraph or members list, adjusted where the minutes note that a member left, returned or declared a conflict of interest.

Mover and seconder

The minutes introduce each motion with “MOVED BY COUNCILLOR … AND SECONDED”. The mover is shown exactly as recorded. The seconder is not named in the minutes, so it is shown as not available. The mover is never inferred.

Limits

  • Council-in-Committee, standing committee, Public Hearing and closed meetings are not included.
  • Two sets of minutes from early 2023 are scanned images, so their text was read with character recognition and may contain small errors.
  • When a member stepped out briefly and returned, they are counted as absent for the votes between the two notes in the minutes.
  • Each reading of a bylaw is its own vote.

Several votes under one agenda heading

When more than one vote is recorded under the same agenda heading at a meeting (for example an amendment, then the main motion, or several readings of a bylaw), each is labelled “Vote 2 of 3 under this agenda heading” in the order they appear in the minutes. The grouping is done by software from the heading. Votes under general headings such as “Bylaws” or “Correspondence” are not numbered, and now and then unrelated motions share a heading; the linked minutes show the full sequence.

Links, downloads and corrections

Every vote and every councillor’s record has its own link: use “Copy link to this vote” or “Copy link to this record” to share it. A link to a vote identifies it by its date, title, text and result, so if a vote is later corrected an old link may no longer find it.

The full table behind this page can be downloaded as a spreadsheet file (CSV, opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets). Changes made after a mistake was found are listed in the corrections log. Terms such as amendment, referral and consent agenda are explained in How to read a vote.

Types of motion

Each vote is labelled Main motion, Amendment, Referral, Postponement, Reconsideration, Procedural or Other, based on how the minutes describe it and on the wording of the motion. “Procedural” here mostly means receiving a report for information. The labels are assigned by this site and are a guide; the motion text is shown in full.

How this page was made

The data was compiled with AI assistance. Software read each set of minutes and extracted the votes, motion text, movers and seconders; the results were checked against samples of the originals, not vote by vote. This page may therefore contain transcription or processing errors.
