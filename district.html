District of North Vancouver · Council term 2022–2026

How each Councillor voted

Independent summary, not affiliated with the District of North Vancouver. Every recorded Council vote from November 2022 to July 2026, taken from the District's official voting-decisions register, which links each vote to its minutes and meeting video. Items with no vote taken (information reports, presentations) are left out.

Summary

Key findings

  • Most votes are unanimous. About 4 in 5 recorded votes had no one opposed. The differences between members show up in the remaining split votes.
  • Lisa Muri and Betty Forbes cast the most "against" votes (about 11% of their votes each) and vote together 72% of the time on split votes. Their dissents are concentrated on rezonings and development: OCP amendments, the Transit-Oriented Areas bylaw, reduced parking near transit, and projects such as the Capilano Road, Mount Seymour Parkway townhouse, Hunter Street and Lynn Valley mixed-use applications.
  • Jordan Back and Catherine Pope are the closest pair on Council (81% agreement on split votes), often joined by Jim Hanson. That trio voted against the April 2026 motion saying Council does not support SSMUH (small-scale multi-unit housing) as set out in Bill 25, against the 2025 OCP Update bylaws, and against upholding two business-licence denials.
  • Jim Hanson sits between the two groups. He votes with Back and Pope on housing policy, but joins Forbes and Muri against a number of specific rezonings, including the Mount Seymour Parkway townhouses, Old Dollarton Road and Lynn Valley Road mixed-use.
  • Mayor Mike Little, Jordan Back and Herman Mah are most often on the winning side of split votes (about 78–79%). Forbes and Muri are on the losing side most often (about 56–58%).
  • Herman Mah is often the lone dissenter (8 times), across mixed topics: parks, transportation, finance and social housing. Muri (10) and Forbes (9) have the most solo "no" votes.
  • Housing, density and development are the main dividing lines. About two-thirds of all split votes (82 of 127) fall in these categories. Taxes and spending, public services and governance are almost always unanimous. Neighbourhood-character issues such as fireworks, coach houses and RV parking split Council, but not along the usual lines.

Method

How to read this

Source. The District of North Vancouver's Council voting decisions register, retrieved 28 September 2026. It covers Regular and Special meetings and Council workshops from 7 November 2022 to 20 July 2026. Closed-meeting votes appear only if Council later released them.

"Against" means against that motion. Some motions are amendments, referrals or motions to reject something. A "no" on a motion to abandon a rezoning is effectively support for the project. Check the motion text (expand any row) before drawing conclusions about a member's position on an issue.

Coverage. The last four years reach back to September 2022, but this Council was sworn in on 7 November 2022. Votes by the previous Council in September and October 2022 are not in this register.

Categories. Each vote is sorted by the District's own tags plus keywords in the motion title. A vote can sit in several categories. A handful are judgment calls; expand a vote in the log to see where it was placed.

Privacy. Names of private residents (property owners and business-licence applicants) are removed from the motion text here. The linked minutes are the full official record.

Data quality. The register is maintained by District staff. A few entries have titles that don't match the motion text. The linked minutes are the official record.
